# Cocoa traceability and claims integrity — a working model

An interactive model of how a commodity processor can govern what it tells customers
about its supply chain, and prove it when an assurance provider asks.

**View the model:** https://USERNAME.github.io/REPO/
**Methodology note:** [basis-of-preparation.pdf](basis-of-preparation.pdf)

---

## What this is

Cocoa is the vehicle, not the subject. The model works through a European cocoa
processor because that supply chain concentrates every hard problem in one place:
hundreds of thousands of smallholders, physical commingling at aggregation, a
regulatory deadline with no tolerance for averages, and branded customers who need
numbers they can put into their own assured disclosures.

Five modules:

| Module | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| The position | Can this company say what it is currently saying? |
| Traceability | Where is the exposure, and what can be done before the deadline? |
| Claims and balance | Has it promised more than it holds? |
| Carbon consequence | Why should anyone fund this? |
| Governance and customers | Who decides, who signs, and what happens on Monday? |

## The central argument

A claim is a description of a physical system. A company can only say what its chain
of custody can carry.

The model makes that concrete with a chain of custody ladder — identity preserved,
mixed identity preserved, segregated, mass balance — and shows what each rung does
and does not support. It then finds the gap that opens when an organisation quotes
a number about mapped *farms* to answer a question about traceable *volume*. Those
are different achievements, and the difference is where compliance exposure and
overstated claims both live.

## Data

**All quantitative data is synthetic.** There is no client and no company. The
figures are constructed to demonstrate a method, and the
[basis of preparation](basis-of-preparation.pdf) derives every one of them, tagged
as either an assumed input or a calculated output.

What is real: the regulatory dates and obligations, the chain of custody tier
definitions, and the structural facts of cocoa supply. These are cited as published
and were current at the version date.

## Transferability

The architecture is sector-neutral — a chain of custody test, a claims register with
owners and expiry dates, a balance control struck at the grain the attribution rule
requires, documented methodology positions, and decision rights with one accountable
party per decision. The same five components have been applied by the author to
malting and barley supply, and apply equally to dairy, palm, textiles, packaging or
construction materials.

## Built with

A single self-contained HTML file. No framework, no build step, no dependencies
beyond two web fonts. Charts are hand-built SVG.
